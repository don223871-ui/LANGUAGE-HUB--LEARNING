# 📖 READING · MICRO-SKILLS MASTER ARCHITECTURE

> **Final structural layer:** Area → Sub-skill → Micro-skill → Learning Detail.
>
> A Micro-skill is the smallest meaningful teachable reading ability in this architecture. It states a concrete learner capability rather than merely naming a topic.

## How to use this map

For every Micro-skill, the teaching unit should eventually answer: **What is it? · Why does it matter? · What does the learner notice/know? · What does the learner do? · How is it practised? · What errors are likely? · How is mastery checked? · How does it transfer?**

# 01 · READING FOUNDATIONS

## 01.1 Word Recognition
- **Orthographic pattern recognition** — recognise recurring spelling patterns quickly.
- **Whole-word recognition** — identify familiar words without laborious letter-by-letter decoding.
- **Morphological recognition** — notice roots, prefixes and suffixes as meaningful visual units.
- **Word-family recognition** — connect related forms such as decide/decision/decisive.
- **Automatic recognition** — reduce conscious processing time for frequent vocabulary.
- **Multiword-unit recognition** — recognise common chunks rather than isolated words.

## 01.2 Word Segmentation
- **Word-boundary detection** — distinguish individual written words accurately.
- **Phrase grouping** — group words into meaningful units.
- **Function-word processing** — process articles, auxiliaries, pronouns and prepositions without losing the sentence frame.
- **Chunk boundary awareness** — recognise where one meaningful unit ends and another begins.
- **Prosodic grouping from print** — use punctuation and syntax to anticipate natural grouping.

## 01.3 Sentence Processing
- **Subject–verb identification** — identify the core proposition.
- **Object/complement recognition** — identify what completes the proposition.
- **Clause boundary recognition** — separate main and subordinate clauses.
- **Syntactic relationship tracking** — understand who did what to whom.
- **Modifier attachment** — connect adjectives, adverbs and phrases to the correct element.
- **Sentence paraphrase** — restate sentence meaning without changing relationships.
- **Complex-sentence unpacking** — simplify dense syntax while preserving meaning.

## 01.4 Reading Fluency
- **Accuracy** — minimise decoding and recognition errors.
- **Automaticity** — process familiar forms with little conscious effort.
- **Phrasing** — read/interpret words in meaningful groups.
- **Pacing** — adjust speed to text difficulty and purpose.
- **Rereading control** — reread strategically rather than automatically.
- **Meaning maintenance** — preserve comprehension while increasing efficiency.

# 02 · LITERAL COMPREHENSION

## 02.1 Locating Explicit Information
- **Question-target identification** — determine exactly what information is requested.
- **Keyword matching** — locate obvious textual matches.
- **Paraphrase matching** — recognise the same idea in different wording.
- **Sentence-level retrieval** — extract the exact relevant statement.
- **Evidence confirmation** — verify the answer against surrounding context.

## 02.2 Identifying Specific Details
- **Fact extraction** — retrieve names, places, dates, quantities and conditions.
- **Example identification** — locate examples supporting an idea.
- **Detail discrimination** — separate relevant details from distractors.
- **Number/data retrieval** — locate and interpret stated figures.
- **Condition tracking** — notice restrictions such as if, unless, only and except.

## 02.3 Following Instructions
- **Sequence recognition** — identify the order of actions.
- **Imperative interpretation** — understand direct instructions.
- **Condition interpretation** — understand when an instruction applies.
- **Procedure tracking** — connect steps across a passage.
- **Completion checking** — verify that every required step is represented.

---

# 03 · MAIN IDEA & GIST

## 03.1 Topic Identification
- **Topic-word recognition** — identify repeated conceptual vocabulary.
- **Subject identification** — state what the passage is about.
- **Topic/detail distinction** — avoid selecting a narrow example as the topic.
- **Topic prediction** — anticipate content from headings and opening material.

## 03.2 Main Idea
- **Central-message identification** — find the idea that controls the paragraph/text.
- **Supporting-detail exclusion** — reject examples or minor facts as the main idea.
- **Scope matching** — choose a main idea broad enough but not too broad.
- **Main-idea paraphrase** — express the central message independently.
- **Paragraph-to-text integration** — combine paragraph ideas into a whole-text message.

## 03.3 Gist Summarising
- **Essential-information selection** — keep only information necessary for the gist.
- **Compression** — reduce length without losing the central meaning.
- **Generalisation** — combine related details into a higher-level statement.
- **One-sentence gist** — express the overall message concisely.
- **Gist verification** — check that the summary represents the text fairly.

---

# 04 · SUPPORTING DETAILS

## 04.1 Examples & Illustrations
- **Example-signal recognition** — notice for example, such as, including and similar cues.
- **Example-to-claim linking** — connect an example to the idea it illustrates.
- **Representative-example identification** — recognise what an example demonstrates.
- **Example relevance judgement** — decide whether an example actually supports the point.

## 04.2 Reasons & Explanations
- **Reason identification** — identify why something is claimed.
- **Cause identification** — recognise what produces an event or condition.
- **Explanation tracking** — follow how or why something works.
- **Reason–claim mapping** — connect support to the proposition it supports.
- **Multiple-reason integration** — combine several supporting reasons.

## 04.3 Evidence & Data
- **Evidence identification** — distinguish evidence from commentary.
- **Data interpretation** — understand stated numbers and comparisons.
- **Evidence–claim linking** — connect evidence to the proposition supported.
- **Evidence relevance** — judge whether evidence actually bears on the claim.
- **Data limitation awareness** — notice what the data does and does not establish.

---

# 05 · SKIMMING

## 05.1 Text Preview
- **Title scanning** — predict broad subject and direction.
- **Heading scanning** — identify major sections.
- **Layout reading** — use paragraphs, lists, captions and formatting as structural clues.
- **Opening/closing inspection** — use high-value positions to form an initial model.
- **Prediction formation** — create a tentative content hypothesis.

## 05.2 Paragraph Skimming
- **Topic-sentence detection** — identify likely paragraph focus.
- **Repeated-keyword detection** — track concepts across the paragraph.
- **First/last-sentence strategy** — use likely high-information positions efficiently.
- **Paragraph-gist extraction** — state each paragraph's function or message.
- **Distractor resistance** — avoid becoming trapped by interesting minor details.

## 05.3 Structural Skimming
- **Section-role identification** — identify introduction, development, contrast, conclusion etc.
- **Discourse-pattern recognition** — recognise cause-effect, problem-solution, chronology and comparison.
- **Argument-outline construction** — build a quick mental/textual map.
- **Information-priority judgement** — decide which sections deserve closer reading.
- **Skim-to-read transition** — move from global overview to targeted close reading.

---

# 06 · SCANNING

## 06.1 Target Identification
- **Question decomposition** — break the task into searchable information.
- **Target-type recognition** — predict whether the answer is a name, number, reason, place, etc.
- **Keyword selection** — choose distinctive search terms.
- **Synonym prediction** — anticipate alternative wording.

## 06.2 Keyword & Synonym Search
- **Visual keyword search** — rapidly locate distinctive forms.
- **Concept search** — search for meaning rather than exact words.
- **Morphological variation recognition** — connect different forms of a word.
- **Paraphrase recognition** — match question wording to source wording.
- **Search-path control** — avoid reading irrelevant sections in full.

## 06.3 Verification
- **Context-window reading** — read enough surrounding text to confirm meaning.
- **Distractor detection** — reject plausible-looking but incorrect matches.
- **Answer-condition checking** — verify quantity, scope and conditions.
- **Question-fit checking** — confirm that the located information answers the exact question.

---

# 07 · VOCABULARY IN CONTEXT

## 07.1 Context Clues
- **Definition clues** — infer meaning from an explicit explanation.
- **Example clues** — infer meaning from an illustration.
- **Contrast clues** — use opposites or contrasts to narrow meaning.
- **Cause/effect clues** — use consequences to infer meaning.
- **Syntactic clues** — use grammatical category and position.
- **Collocational clues** — use surrounding words.
- **Context-window integration** — combine multiple clues rather than relying on one.

## 07.2 Collocation & Semantic Relations
- **Collocation recognition** — identify natural word partnerships.
- **Synonym recognition** — connect related meanings.
- **Antonym recognition** — use contrastive meaning.
- **Word-family recognition** — infer relationships among forms.
- **Semantic-field mapping** — connect vocabulary around a shared concept.
- **Sense disambiguation** — choose the contextually appropriate meaning.

## 07.3 Register & Connotation
- **Register recognition** — distinguish formal, neutral and informal choices.
- **Connotation recognition** — identify positive, negative or marked associations.
- **Evaluative vocabulary detection** — recognise words carrying judgement.
- **Context-sensitive interpretation** — distinguish dictionary meaning from contextual force.
- **Writer-choice interpretation** — connect lexical choice to purpose and stance.

---

# 08 · INFERENCE & IMPLICATION

## 08.1 Combining Clues
- **Clue extraction** — identify evidence relevant to an inference.
- **Cross-sentence integration** — combine information from separate sentences.
- **Logical connection** — connect clues without inventing unsupported information.
- **Inference strength judgement** — distinguish strong from weak inferences.

## 08.2 Writer Attitude Inference
- **Evaluative-language detection** — identify approval/disapproval signals.
- **Modal-language interpretation** — interpret certainty and caution.
- **Framing detection** — notice how presentation shapes attitude.
- **Attitude-evidence linking** — justify an inferred attitude with textual evidence.

## 08.3 Implication
- **Indirect-meaning detection** — identify what is suggested rather than said.
- **Pragmatic interpretation** — understand meaning created by context.
- **Consequence inference** — identify what logically follows.
- **Implicit-message paraphrase** — state the implied meaning clearly.

## 08.4 Inference vs Fact
- **Explicit/inferred distinction** — separate textual evidence from interpretation.
- **Evidence threshold judgement** — ask whether an inference is sufficiently supported.
- **Alternative-inference testing** — consider whether another interpretation fits.
- **Unsupported-inference rejection** — avoid assumptions not licensed by the text.

---

# 09 · REFERENCE & COHESION

## 09.1 Pronoun Reference
- **Antecedent identification** — determine what a pronoun refers to.
- **Reference-chain tracking** — follow an entity across sentences.
- **Ambiguity resolution** — use syntax and meaning to resolve unclear reference.
- **Demonstrative reference** — interpret this/that/these/those in context.

## 09.2 Linking Devices
- **Addition recognition** — interpret additive relationships.
- **Contrast recognition** — identify opposing or unexpected relationships.
- **Cause/result recognition** — identify logical consequence.
- **Sequence recognition** — follow chronological or procedural links.
- **Concession recognition** — understand although/however-type relationships.

## 09.3 Lexical Cohesion
- **Repetition tracking** — follow repeated key terms.
- **Synonym-chain tracking** — connect lexical alternatives.
- **Superordinate/subordinate relations** — connect general and specific terms.
- **Semantic-chain mapping** — follow a concept across a text.
- **Cohesion-to-coherence connection** — use linguistic links to build whole-text meaning.

---

# 10 · TEXT ORGANISATION

## 10.1 Paragraph Structure
- **Topic-sentence function** — identify paragraph orientation.
- **Development-sentence function** — identify explanation, evidence and example roles.
- **Concluding function** — recognise closure or transition.
- **Sentence-role labelling** — classify each sentence by function.
- **Paragraph coherence checking** — determine whether sentences develop the same controlling idea.

## 10.2 Text Patterns
- **Chronological structure** — follow time/order development.
- **Cause-effect structure** — map causes and consequences.
- **Problem-solution structure** — identify problem, response and result.
- **Compare-contrast structure** — map similarities and differences.
- **Classification structure** — identify categories and members.
- **Argument structure** — follow claim, support, counterargument and conclusion.

## 10.3 Information Flow
- **Given/new information tracking** — follow how information develops.
- **Topic progression** — track changing paragraph topics.
- **Thematic progression** — connect new information to previous themes.
- **Transition prediction** — anticipate what information is likely to come next.
- **Whole-text flow mapping** — construct a coherent information map.

---

# 11 · WRITER'S PURPOSE

## 11.1 Communicative Purpose
- **Informing** — recognise factual information delivery.
- **Explaining** — recognise attempts to make a process/idea understandable.
- **Describing** — recognise depiction of people, places, events or states.
- **Arguing** — recognise claim-and-support structures.
- **Persuading** — recognise attempts to influence beliefs or action.
- **Evaluating** — recognise judgement or assessment.

## 11.2 Audience Awareness
- **Audience identification** — infer intended readership.
- **Knowledge-assumption detection** — notice what the writer assumes readers know.
- **Register-audience connection** — link language choice to audience.
- **Information-selection interpretation** — understand why some information is foregrounded or omitted.
- **Genre-audience recognition** — connect text type with expected readers.

---

# 12 · TONE, ATTITUDE & STANCE

## 12.1 Tone
- **Tone-vocabulary recognition** — distinguish neutral, critical, enthusiastic, cautious, humorous and similar qualities.
- **Tone-evidence identification** — locate linguistic clues supporting the judgement.
- **Tone-shift detection** — notice changes within a text.
- **Tone comparison** — distinguish similar but not identical tones.

## 12.2 Stance & Certainty
- **Hedging recognition** — interpret cautious commitment.
- **Boosting recognition** — interpret strong commitment.
- **Modality interpretation** — understand possibility, necessity and probability.
- **Source-attribution tracking** — distinguish writer commitment from reported claims.
- **Claim-strength scaling** — place statements on a certainty continuum.

## 12.3 Evaluation
- **Value-judgement detection** — identify what is presented as good/bad, desirable/problematic.
- **Positive/negative framing** — recognise evaluative framing.
- **Loaded-language detection** — identify emotionally or ideologically marked wording.
- **Evaluation-target identification** — determine what is being judged.
- **Evaluation justification** — support interpretation with textual evidence.

---

# 13 · FACT, OPINION & CLAIM

## 13.1 Fact vs Opinion
- **Verifiability judgement** — determine whether a statement can in principle be checked.
- **Subjectivity detection** — identify personal judgement or preference.
- **Mixed-statement recognition** — identify statements containing both evidence and evaluation.
- **Evidence requirement awareness** — understand what would be needed to verify a claim.

## 13.2 Claims & Generalisations
- **Claim identification** — locate propositions the writer wants accepted.
- **Claim paraphrase** — restate the proposition accurately.
- **Scope recognition** — identify who/what/how widely the claim applies.
- **Generalisation detection** — notice broad conclusions from limited information.
- **Claim hierarchy** — distinguish central and subsidiary claims.

## 13.3 Qualification & Limitation
- **Qualifier detection** — recognise words limiting strength or scope.
- **Scope limitation** — identify boundaries of a claim.
- **Exception detection** — notice conditions that restrict generalisation.
- **Strength recalibration** — interpret the claim at its actual level rather than exaggerating it.

---

# 14 · ARGUMENT & EVIDENCE

## 14.1 Claim–Reason–Evidence
- **Claim extraction** — identify the proposition being defended.
- **Reason identification** — identify why the claim is presented as true.
- **Evidence identification** — locate supporting facts/examples/data.
- **Support mapping** — connect evidence through reasons to claims.
- **Argument reconstruction** — restate the argument in logical order.

## 14.2 Counterargument & Concession
- **Alternative-position recognition** — identify another viewpoint.
- **Concession recognition** — notice where the writer grants part of an opposing point.
- **Rebuttal recognition** — identify the response against the opposing view.
- **Position comparison** — compare strength and assumptions of both sides.
- **Argument-shift tracking** — follow how the writer returns to the main position.

## 14.3 Evidence Quality
- **Relevance judgement** — determine whether evidence bears directly on the claim.
- **Sufficiency judgement** — determine whether there is enough support.
- **Credibility awareness** — consider source quality and reliability.
- **Specificity judgement** — distinguish precise evidence from vague assertion.
- **Evidence limitation recognition** — identify what evidence cannot prove.

---

# 15 · CRITICAL READING

## 15.1 Assumptions
- **Implicit-premise detection** — identify what must be accepted for an argument to work.
- **Assumption testing** — ask whether the premise is justified.
- **Alternative-premise construction** — consider other explanations.
- **Assumption–claim connection** — show how an unstated premise supports the conclusion.

## 15.2 Bias & Perspective
- **Perspective identification** — identify the viewpoint from which the issue is presented.
- **Selection bias detection** — notice what information is included or omitted.
- **Framing analysis** — examine how wording influences interpretation.
- **Balance assessment** — judge whether relevant perspectives are represented.
- **Alternative-perspective comparison** — reinterpret the issue from another position.

## 15.3 Credibility & Reliability
- **Source evaluation** — consider expertise, purpose and provenance.
- **Evidence verification mindset** — ask what supports a statement.
- **Internal-consistency checking** — detect contradictions.
- **External-consistency awareness** — compare with independent information when available.
- **Confidence calibration** — assign an appropriate level of trust rather than binary true/false judgement.

## 15.4 Comparing Sources
- **Agreement mapping** — identify shared claims.
- **Contradiction mapping** — identify conflicting claims.
- **Emphasis comparison** — identify what each source foregrounds.
- **Omission comparison** — notice what one source leaves out.
- **Framing comparison** — compare how the same issue is represented.
- **Source-purpose comparison** — explain differences through audience/purpose.

---

# 16 · RESPONSE & SYNTHESIS

## 16.1 Summarising
- **Main-point selection** — retain central information.
- **Detail reduction** — remove low-value information.
- **Information grouping** — combine related points.
- **Neutral restatement** — avoid adding personal judgement.
- **Accuracy check** — confirm that the summary does not distort the source.
- **Compression control** — adjust summary length to the task.

## 16.2 Paraphrasing
- **Lexical substitution** — change vocabulary appropriately.
- **Grammatical transformation** — change structure while preserving meaning.
- **Clause restructuring** — reorganise information.
- **Collocation control** — use natural alternative combinations.
- **Meaning preservation** — ensure no information is added, lost or distorted.
- **Originality check** — avoid superficial word replacement.

## 16.3 Cross-Text Synthesis
- **Shared-theme identification** — find the common issue across texts.
- **Agreement synthesis** — combine compatible information.
- **Contrast synthesis** — integrate differences rather than listing them separately.
- **Complementary-evidence integration** — combine information that fills different parts of the picture.
- **Source attribution** — keep track of where each idea comes from.
- **Integrated conclusion** — construct a new understanding from multiple sources.

## 16.4 Note-taking & Knowledge Building
- **Selective noting** — record only useful information.
- **Hierarchical organisation** — distinguish main ideas, support and examples.
- **Keyword notation** — reduce notes to efficient prompts.
- **Concept mapping** — represent relationships visually or structurally.
- **Source tagging** — preserve source identity.
- **Retrieval-friendly organisation** — make notes reusable later.

## 16.5 Transfer to Speaking & Writing
- **Evidence transfer** — use reading evidence in a spoken/written response.
- **Vocabulary transfer** — reuse useful contextual vocabulary accurately.
- **Idea development** — turn reading information into an independently developed point.
- **Position formation** — move from understanding a source to forming a justified view.
- **Read-to-speak** — use a text as a basis for discussion.
- **Read-to-write** — use source material as a basis for structured writing.
- **Independent synthesis** — combine source input with the learner's own reasoning.

# 🎯 FINAL READING ARCHITECTURE

**LEVEL 1 — AREA**

16 major Reading domains.

**LEVEL 2 — SUB-SKILL**

Each domain is divided into concrete teachable abilities.

**LEVEL 3 — MICRO-SKILL**

Each Sub-skill is decomposed into the smallest meaningful abilities that can be taught, practised and assessed.

**LEVEL 4 — LEARNING DETAIL (NOT ANOTHER STRUCTURAL LEVEL)**

Every Micro-skill can now be taught through:

1. What it is
2. Why it matters
3. What the learner needs to know
4. What the learner needs to notice
5. What the learner needs to do
6. Guided practice
7. Independent practice
8. Common errors
9. Feedback criteria
10. Mastery check
11. Level progression A1 → C2+
12. Transfer to other skills
13. Final learning outcome

> **This is the stopping point for structural decomposition. We do not create another permanent “micro-micro-skill” layer.**
