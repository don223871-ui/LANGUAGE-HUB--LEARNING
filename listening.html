01 · LANGUAGE SKILLS · 02 · LISTENING

Listening

The ability to process, understand, interpret and respond to spoken language in real time.

LISTENING · MASTER LEARNING MAP

From hearing sound to understanding meaning.

Listening combines sound recognition, language processing, meaning construction, inference, speaker interpretation and interactive response.

01

Listening Foundations

Build accurate perception of speech.

  • Sound discrimination
  • Word recognition
  • Speech segmentation
  • Stress and rhythm awareness
  • Connected-speech recognition
Student can → identify spoken language accurately.
02

Literal Comprehension

Understand explicitly stated spoken information.

  • Key facts
  • Instructions
  • Numbers and details
  • Events and actions
  • Direct questions
Student can → retrieve stated information from speech.
03

Gist & Main Idea

Recognise the overall message of spoken input.

  • Topic
  • Overall message
  • Speaker's central point
  • Section gist
  • Concise summary
Student can → explain what the speaker is mainly saying.
04

Listening for Detail

Extract specific information while processing speech.

  • Names and dates
  • Examples
  • Reasons
  • Qualifications
  • Supporting information
Student can → identify relevant details accurately.
05

Prediction & Anticipation

Use context and language cues to anticipate meaning.

  • Context prediction
  • Topic prediction
  • Lexical anticipation
  • Structural prediction
  • Speaker-intention prediction
Student can → anticipate likely meaning while listening.
06

Inference & Implication

Understand meanings that are implied rather than directly stated.

  • Contextual inference
  • Speaker implication
  • Indirect meaning
  • Attitude inference
  • Background knowledge
Student can → justify what the speaker implies.
07

Vocabulary & Grammar in Speech

Process language forms as they occur in real time.

  • Unknown-word inference
  • Chunks and collocations
  • Grammar cues
  • Reduced forms
  • Meaning from prosody
Student can → use linguistic cues to construct meaning.
08

Organisation & Discourse

Follow how spoken ideas are connected and developed.

  • Signposting
  • Topic shifts
  • Sequencing
  • Cause and contrast
  • Discourse markers
Student can → track the structure of spoken discourse.
09

Speaker Purpose & Intention

Understand what a speaker is trying to accomplish.

  • Inform
  • Explain
  • Persuade
  • Request
  • Warn
  • Suggest
Student can → connect speech to communicative purpose.
10

Tone, Attitude & Stance

Interpret emotion, evaluation and certainty through language and prosody.

  • Intonation
  • Stress
  • Emotion
  • Certainty and hedging
  • Approval and criticism
Student can → identify and justify speaker attitude.
11

Multiple Speakers & Interaction

Manage meaning when several speakers interact.

  • Speaker identification
  • Turn changes
  • Agreement and disagreement
  • Overlapping speech
  • Repair and clarification
Student can → follow who says what and how speakers respond.
12

Critical Listening

Evaluate spoken information rather than simply understand it.

  • Claims
  • Evidence
  • Bias
  • Assumptions
  • Credibility
  • Contradictions
Student can → evaluate spoken messages critically.
13

Note-taking & Synthesis

Capture and reorganise important spoken information.

  • Key-word notes
  • Abbreviation
  • Idea mapping
  • Summarising
  • Combining information
Student can → turn listening input into usable knowledge.
14

Real-time Processing

Maintain comprehension despite speed, variation and incomplete understanding.

  • Selective attention
  • Repair strategies
  • Processing under pressure
  • Tolerance of ambiguity
  • Maintaining the overall message
Student can → continue listening without translating every word.
15

Listening Across Contexts

Adapt comprehension to different speakers and situations.

  • Formal speech
  • Conversation
  • Academic lectures
  • Media
  • Workplace communication
  • Different accents and varieties
Student can → adjust listening strategies to context.
16

Listening-to-Response Transfer

Use what is heard to produce an appropriate response.

  • Answering
  • Clarifying
  • Summarising
  • Discussing
  • Taking action
  • Responding appropriately
Student can → turn listening into effective interaction.
LISTENING DEVELOPMENT PATH

Perceive → Decode → Understand → Interpret → Evaluate → Respond

Advanced listening increasingly becomes automatic, selective and strategic rather than word-by-word decoding.
