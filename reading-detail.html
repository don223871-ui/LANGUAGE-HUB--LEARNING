01 · LANGUAGE SKILLS · READING · DEEP MAP

Reading · Complete Learning Architecture

A detailed map of the sub-skills behind effective reading — what each area teaches, what the learner does, how it is practised, how mastery is checked, and what transferable ability it produces.

HOW TO USE THIS MAP

Area → Sub-skill → Learning Action → Performance → Transfer

Every Reading area below is broken into teachable sub-skills. For each sub-skill, the learner identifies the target, learns the process, practises it with increasing independence, demonstrates performance, and transfers it to authentic reading.

01

Reading Foundations

Build the low-level processes that allow attention to move from visual language to meaningful text without excessive effort.

01.1 Word Recognition

Learn: recognise familiar words accurately and increasingly automatically.

Do: identify words in isolation and in connected text, then read them without prolonged decoding.

Practise: high-frequency words, repeated exposure, timed but accuracy-first reading.

Outcome: faster, more reliable access to word meaning.

01.2 Word Segmentation

Learn: see where words begin and end and process written strings efficiently.

Do: separate unfamiliar strings into recognisable units and confirm meaning from context.

Practise: phrase and sentence reading with controlled difficulty.

Outcome: smoother sentence-level processing.

01.3 Sentence Processing

Learn: connect words into grammatical and meaningful units.

Do: identify who does what, when, where and under what conditions.

Practise: sentence matching, ordering, parsing and meaning checks.

Outcome: accurate understanding of sentence meaning.

01.4 Reading Accuracy

Learn: minimise misread words, skipped information and false interpretations.

Do: notice uncertainty, reread and verify rather than guess carelessly.

Practise: error spotting and controlled rereading.

Outcome: dependable comprehension.

01.5 Reading Fluency

Learn: balance speed, phrasing and comprehension.

Do: read meaningful phrases rather than process every word separately.

Practise: repeated reading, phrase marking and timed comprehension.

Outcome: more automatic reading with maintained meaning.

02

Literal Comprehension

Retrieve information that the writer states directly.

02.1 Locating Explicit Information

Learn: distinguish information actually stated in the text.

Do: find the sentence or passage that answers a question.

Practise: who/what/where/when/how tasks.

Outcome: accurate retrieval without unsupported guessing.

02.2 Identifying Key Facts

Learn: recognise factual details relevant to the reading purpose.

Do: select relevant facts and ignore distractors.

Practise: detail-selection tasks.

Outcome: efficient factual comprehension.

02.3 Following Events & Actions

Learn: track actions, participants and sequence.

Do: reconstruct what happened and in what order.

Practise: sequencing and event maps.

Outcome: coherent understanding of explicit content.

02.4 Answering Text-Based Questions

Learn: answer from textual evidence rather than prior assumptions.

Do: locate, select and paraphrase the relevant information.

Practise: short-answer and evidence-location tasks.

Outcome: evidence-based literal answers.

03

Main Idea & Gist

Identify the central message at text, section and paragraph level.

03.1 Topic Identification

Learn: distinguish what a passage is about from what it says about that topic.

Do: name the topic in a concise phrase.

Practise: title and topic matching.

Outcome: fast orientation to content.

03.2 Main Idea

Learn: identify the most important message.

Do: state the idea in your own words.

Practise: main-idea selection and justification.

Outcome: central meaning rather than detail accumulation.

03.3 Paragraph Gist

Learn: identify what each paragraph contributes.

Do: assign a short gist statement.

Practise: paragraph headings and one-sentence summaries.

Outcome: structural comprehension.

03.4 Summarising Central Meaning

Learn: compress information without losing the core message.

Do: remove repetition and minor detail.

Practise: progressively shorter summaries.

Outcome: transferable summarising ability.

04

Supporting Details

Understand how examples, reasons, evidence and explanations develop central ideas.

04.1 Identifying Supporting Information

Learn: distinguish central ideas from information that develops them.

Do: label examples, reasons and explanations.

Outcome: clearer text hierarchy.

04.2 Detail-to-Idea Connection

Learn: understand what a detail supports or explains.

Do: connect each detail to its parent idea.

Practise: matching and evidence maps.

Outcome: integrated comprehension.

04.3 Relevant vs Irrelevant Detail

Learn: select information according to reading purpose.

Do: justify why a detail matters.

Outcome: selective reading.

04.4 Examples, Reasons & Explanations

Learn: recognise different functions of supporting information.

Do: classify the relationship between a claim and its support.

Outcome: deeper paragraph comprehension.

05

Skimming

Read selectively to establish global meaning and text structure.

05.1 Text Preview

Learn: use title, headings, layout and opening information as predictive cues.

Do: form a preliminary hypothesis before close reading.

Outcome: purposeful entry into a text.

05.2 Topic-Sentence Reading

Learn: locate high-value information quickly.

Do: sample beginnings and endings of paragraphs and verify the gist.

Outcome: rapid structural understanding.

05.3 Keyword Tracking

Learn: follow repeated and semantically related words.

Do: trace the text's key concepts.

Outcome: faster identification of themes.

05.4 Global Gist Under Time

Learn: prioritise comprehension over exhaustive decoding.

Do: explain the text's purpose and structure after limited reading.

Outcome: strategic speed.

06

Scanning

Search efficiently for specific information.

06.1 Target Definition

Learn: know exactly what information you are looking for.

Do: convert a question into a search target.

Outcome: focused attention.

06.2 Visual Search

Learn: move through a text using likely visual and lexical cues.

Do: scan names, numbers, dates, headings and distinctive words.

Outcome: faster retrieval.

06.3 Synonym Scanning

Learn: recognise that the answer may not repeat the question's wording.

Do: search for paraphrases and related expressions.

Outcome: stronger exam and real-world retrieval.

06.4 Verification

Learn: distinguish a plausible match from the correct match.

Do: read enough surrounding context to confirm.

Outcome: accurate targeted reading.

07

Vocabulary in Context

Construct word meaning from textual evidence and linguistic relationships.

07.1 Context Clues

Learn: use nearby information to narrow meaning.

Do: identify definition, example, contrast and cause clues.

Outcome: independent meaning construction.

07.2 Word-Class Clues

Learn: use grammatical position to identify likely meaning and function.

Do: identify noun, verb, adjective or adverb roles and test interpretations.

Outcome: more precise lexical inference.

07.3 Collocation & Semantic Relations

Learn: use surrounding words and semantic fields.

Do: infer meaning from recurring combinations and contrasts.

Outcome: contextual rather than isolated vocabulary knowledge.

07.4 Meaning Selection

Learn: choose the contextually appropriate sense of a word.

Do: compare possible meanings and reject those that do not fit.

Outcome: flexible vocabulary comprehension.

07.5 Vocabulary Transfer

Learn: move from recognition to usable lexical knowledge.

Do: record collocations, register, grammar patterns and examples.

Outcome: reading becomes input for speaking and writing.

08

Inference & Implication

Build justified meanings that are not stated directly.

08.1 Identifying Explicit Clues

Learn: distinguish evidence from interpretation.

Do: underline the exact clues that support a conclusion.

Outcome: evidence-based inference.

08.2 Combining Clues

Learn: integrate information across sentences or paragraphs.

Do: connect multiple clues before deciding what they imply.

Outcome: multi-step comprehension.

08.3 Inferring Meaning & Relationships

Learn: infer cause, consequence, relationship, motivation or attitude.

Do: formulate a conclusion and identify supporting evidence.

Outcome: deeper interpretation.

08.4 Implication

Learn: recognise what a writer suggests without saying explicitly.

Do: paraphrase the implied message and distinguish it from literal wording.

Outcome: sensitivity to indirect meaning.

08.5 Inference vs Fact

Learn: separate what the text states from what the reader concludes.

Do: label statements as explicit, inferred or unsupported.

Outcome: disciplined interpretation.

08.6 Strength of Inference

Learn: judge whether evidence strongly or weakly supports a conclusion.

Do: qualify claims with appropriate certainty.

Outcome: critical, cautious reading.

09

Reference & Cohesion

Track how language connects ideas across a text.

09.1 Pronoun Reference

Learn: identify what pronouns and demonstratives refer to.

Do: trace references backward and forward when necessary.

Outcome: connected sentence comprehension.

09.2 Lexical Chains

Learn: recognise repetition, synonyms and related vocabulary as cohesion.

Do: map recurring concepts.

Outcome: clearer topic development.

09.3 Linking Relationships

Learn: interpret addition, contrast, cause, result, condition and concession.

Do: identify the logical relationship between clauses.

Outcome: accurate discourse interpretation.

09.4 Ellipsis & Substitution

Learn: recover omitted or substituted information.

Do: reconstruct the intended meaning from context.

Outcome: stronger comprehension of natural prose.

10

Text Organisation

Understand the architecture through which a writer develops information.

10.1 Paragraph Function

Learn: identify what each paragraph does in the whole text.

Do: label paragraphs as introducing, explaining, contrasting, evidencing, concluding and so on.

Outcome: structural awareness.

10.2 Information Sequence

Learn: track chronological, logical and rhetorical progression.

Do: reconstruct the order of ideas.

Outcome: coherent text mapping.

10.3 Text Patterns

Learn: recognise problem–solution, cause–effect, compare–contrast, classification and explanation.

Do: identify the dominant organisational pattern.

Outcome: prediction of where information is likely to appear.

10.4 Structural Mapping

Learn: represent a long text as connected stages.

Do: create a paragraph/function map.

Outcome: stronger comprehension of complex texts.

11

Writer's Purpose

Connect textual choices with the writer's communicative goal.

11.1 Identifying Primary Purpose

Learn: distinguish whether the text mainly informs, explains, argues, evaluates, describes or persuades.

Do: justify the purpose using textual evidence.

Outcome: purposeful reading.

11.2 Audience Awareness

Learn: infer who the text is designed for.

Do: connect vocabulary, tone and assumed knowledge to audience.

Outcome: contextual interpretation.

11.3 Communicative Intent

Learn: understand what response the writer seeks.

Do: identify whether the writer wants the reader to know, believe, feel or do something.

Outcome: deeper purpose analysis.

11.4 Purpose Across Sections

Learn: recognise that individual paragraphs can have local purposes within a larger goal.

Do: map section function to overall purpose.

Outcome: whole-text understanding.

12

Tone, Attitude & Stance

Interpret evaluation, emotion, certainty and positioning in written language.

12.1 Tone Recognition

Learn: recognise the overall emotional or evaluative quality of prose.

Do: select precise tone labels and support them with wording.

Outcome: nuanced interpretation.

12.2 Attitude

Learn: identify approval, criticism, concern, enthusiasm, scepticism and related positions.

Do: trace evaluative vocabulary and rhetorical choices.

Outcome: evidence-based attitude interpretation.

12.3 Stance & Certainty

Learn: recognise how strongly a writer commits to a proposition.

Do: interpret modal verbs, hedges, boosters and reporting language.

Outcome: accurate interpretation of certainty.

12.4 Register & Voice

Learn: recognise formal, neutral, conversational, academic and journalistic choices.

Do: connect style with purpose and audience.

Outcome: sensitivity to register.

13

Fact, Opinion & Claim

Distinguish information types and understand the status of statements.

13.1 Fact Recognition

Learn: identify statements presented as verifiable information.

Do: distinguish factual content from interpretation.

Outcome: more precise reading.

13.2 Opinion & Evaluation

Learn: recognise personal or evaluative judgement.

Do: identify language signalling evaluation.

Outcome: awareness of subjectivity.

13.3 Claims

Learn: identify propositions a writer asks the reader to accept.

Do: restate the claim and locate its support.

Outcome: argument awareness.

13.4 Qualification & Limitation

Learn: recognise when a statement is narrowed, hedged or conditional.

Do: preserve the exact strength of the writer's claim.

Outcome: avoidance of overgeneralisation.

14

Argument & Evidence

Reconstruct reasoning and judge the support provided for claims.

14.1 Claim Identification

Learn: locate the central proposition in an argument.

Do: distinguish claim from background information.

Outcome: clear argument structure.

14.2 Reasoning

Learn: understand why the writer thinks a claim follows.

Do: connect reasons to claims.

Outcome: logical comprehension.

14.3 Evidence

Learn: identify examples, statistics, research, quotations and observations used as support.

Do: connect evidence to the proposition it supports.

Outcome: evidence literacy.

14.4 Counterargument & Concession

Learn: recognise opposing views and strategic acknowledgement.

Do: explain how a concession changes or strengthens the argument.

Outcome: sophisticated argument comprehension.

14.5 Strength & Relevance

Learn: judge whether evidence is relevant and sufficiently strong.

Do: explain limitations without confusing them with total rejection.

Outcome: reasoned evaluation.

15

Critical Reading

Evaluate texts as constructed arguments rather than simply absorb information.

15.1 Questioning Assumptions

Learn: identify ideas the writer treats as given.

Do: ask what must be true for the argument to work.

Outcome: independent questioning.

15.2 Bias & Perspective

Learn: recognise selective framing and perspective.

Do: compare wording, omissions and emphasis.

Outcome: awareness of textual positioning.

15.3 Credibility

Learn: evaluate source, evidence and expertise.

Do: distinguish credible support from weak authority signals.

Outcome: informed trust.

15.4 Comparing Sources

Learn: compare claims, evidence, framing and conclusions across texts.

Do: identify agreement, contradiction and complementary evidence.

Outcome: multi-source critical reading.

15.5 Evaluative Response

Learn: form a judgement that can be defended with evidence.

Do: state a judgement, cite textual support and qualify it.

Outcome: independent critical response.

16

Response & Synthesis

Transform reading input into new understanding and language production.

16.1 Summarising

Learn: compress key information while preserving meaning.

Do: select, combine and paraphrase main ideas.

Outcome: concise knowledge representation.

16.2 Paraphrasing

Learn: express source meaning using independent language.

Do: change structure, wording and grammar while preserving meaning.

Outcome: flexible language control.

16.3 Cross-Text Synthesis

Learn: combine information from multiple texts.

Do: group ideas by theme, agreement, contrast or evidence.

Outcome: integrated understanding.

16.4 Evidence Transfer

Learn: use reading evidence in speaking and writing.

Do: select, attribute, paraphrase and integrate relevant information.

Outcome: reading becomes productive knowledge.

16.5 Independent Response

Learn: move from text-dependent answers to an informed personal or academic response.

Do: combine comprehension, evaluation and original reasoning.

Outcome: independent literacy.

MASTER LEARNING STANDARD

WHAT → WHY → KNOW → DO → PRACTISE → PERFORM → TRANSFER → ASSESS

Across all 16 areas, the final target is not knowing terminology. The learner must demonstrate observable reading behaviour: accurate comprehension, strategic processing, justified interpretation, critical evaluation and transfer into speaking, writing and independent learning.
