01 · LANGUAGE SKILLS · 01 · READING

Reading

A complete map of the knowledge, processes and sub-skills a learner develops to understand, interpret, evaluate and respond to written language.

READING · MASTER LEARNING MAP

From decoding text to critical understanding.

Reading is not one skill. It is a network of processes that move from recognising language to constructing meaning, interpreting the writer's message and responding independently.

01

Reading Foundations

Build the basic processes that make written language accessible.

  • Letter–sound and word recognition
  • Word segmentation
  • High-frequency word recognition
  • Sentence-level processing
  • Reading speed and accuracy
Student learns → recognise written language efficiently and accurately.
02

Literal Comprehension

Understand information that is directly stated in the text.

  • Identify stated information
  • Locate key facts
  • Identify people, places, events and actions
  • Follow explicit descriptions
  • Answer who / what / where / when / how questions
Student can → retrieve information directly from a text.
03

Main Idea & Gist

Recognise what a text, paragraph or section is mainly about.

  • Global meaning
  • Topic vs main idea
  • Paragraph purpose
  • Summarising central points
  • Distinguishing main ideas from minor information
Student can → state the central message concisely.
04

Supporting Details

Identify the information that develops, explains or supports an idea.

  • Examples
  • Reasons
  • Evidence
  • Explanations
  • Descriptions and qualifications
Student can → connect details to the ideas they support.
05

Skimming

Read selectively and rapidly to establish overall meaning and structure.

  • Use titles and headings
  • Preview paragraphs
  • Notice topic sentences
  • Track repeated keywords
  • Form a quick global hypothesis
Student can → decide what a text is about without reading every word.
06

Scanning

Search a text efficiently for specific information.

  • Names and numbers
  • Dates and places
  • Keywords and synonyms
  • Specific facts
  • Targeted information retrieval
Student can → locate relevant information quickly.
07

Vocabulary in Context

Use the surrounding text to understand unfamiliar language.

  • Context clues
  • Synonym and contrast clues
  • Examples and definitions
  • Word class and grammatical clues
  • Collocation and semantic relationships
  • Meaning beyond dictionary definitions
Student can → infer a plausible meaning from context.
08

Inference & Implication

Construct meaning that is suggested rather than directly stated.

  • Combine textual clues
  • Infer unstated information
  • Interpret implications
  • Distinguish inference from fact
  • Use background knowledge appropriately
Student can → explain what the text implies and justify the inference.
09

Reference & Cohesion

Track how words and ideas connect across sentences and paragraphs.

  • Pronoun reference
  • Substitution and ellipsis
  • Lexical repetition and chains
  • Linking expressions
  • Cause, contrast, addition and result
Student can → follow connections instead of interpreting sentences in isolation.
10

Text Organisation

Recognise how information is structured and developed.

  • Paragraph structure
  • Topic and supporting sentences
  • Chronological order
  • Problem–solution
  • Cause–effect
  • Compare–contrast
  • Classification and explanation
Student can → map how a text moves from one idea to another.
11

Writer's Purpose

Identify why the writer has produced the text and what they want to achieve.

  • Inform
  • Explain
  • Describe
  • Argue
  • Persuade
  • Evaluate
  • Entertain or engage
Student can → connect language choices to communicative purpose.
12

Tone, Attitude & Stance

Interpret the writer's position, feelings and degree of certainty.

  • Positive / negative evaluation
  • Certainty and uncertainty
  • Approval and criticism
  • Formal and informal stance
  • Emphasis and hedging
  • Attitude vocabulary
Student can → identify and support claims about the writer's attitude.
13

Fact, Opinion & Claim

Distinguish types of statements and understand what the writer is asserting.

  • Fact vs opinion
  • Claims
  • Interpretations
  • Predictions
  • Generalisation
  • Qualification and limitation
Student can → classify statements and explain the difference.
14

Argument & Evidence

Follow reasoning and evaluate how ideas are supported.

  • Claim → reason → evidence
  • Examples and data
  • Counterarguments
  • Concessions
  • Logical relationships
  • Strength and relevance of support
Student can → reconstruct an argument and judge its support.
15

Critical Reading

Move beyond understanding to independent evaluation of a text.

  • Question assumptions
  • Evaluate evidence
  • Recognise bias and perspective
  • Compare sources
  • Identify gaps and limitations
  • Assess credibility
Student can → respond to a text with reasoned evaluation.
16

Response & Synthesis

Use reading to build, compare and communicate knowledge.

  • Summarise
  • Paraphrase
  • Compare texts
  • Integrate information
  • Take notes
  • Use evidence in speaking and writing
Student can → transfer information from reading into new language use.
THE READING DEVELOPMENT PATH

Recognise → Understand → Interpret → Evaluate → Respond

These stages are not rigid levels. Advanced readers continually move between them, using lower-level processes automatically while higher-level interpretation and evaluation become increasingly sophisticated.

STANDARD FOR EVERY READING AREA

WHAT → WHY → LEARN → PRACTISE → PERFORM → TRANSFER → ASSESS

Each of the 16 areas above is only the top-level map. The next stage is to open them one by one and define their complete sub-skills, learning objectives, task types, progression from A1 to C2, observable performance and real-world transfer.
