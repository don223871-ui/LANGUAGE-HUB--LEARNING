01 · LANGUAGE SKILLS · 03 · SPEAKING

Speaking

The ability to express meaning, interact with others and communicate clearly, accurately, fluently and appropriately.

SPEAKING · MASTER LEARNING MAP

From forming language to managing real communication.

Speaking combines language knowledge, planning, real-time processing, pronunciation, discourse, interaction and communicative strategy.

01

Speaking Foundations

Build the basic resources required for oral production.

  • Word retrieval
  • Sentence formation
  • Pronunciation control
  • Basic formulaic language
  • Turn initiation
Student can → produce understandable basic utterances.
02

Accuracy & Control

Use grammar, vocabulary and forms appropriately.

  • Grammatical accuracy
  • Lexical precision
  • Word choice
  • Self-correction
  • Error monitoring
Student can → express meaning with increasing control.
03

Fluency & Automaticity

Speak with appropriate flow and manageable hesitation.

  • Speech rate
  • Pausing
  • Chunk retrieval
  • Reduced hesitation
  • Spontaneous production
Student can → maintain speech without excessive breakdown.
04

Pronunciation & Intelligibility

Make speech easy enough for listeners to understand.

  • Individual sounds
  • Word stress
  • Sentence stress
  • Rhythm
  • Intonation
  • Connected speech
Student can → communicate intelligibly across contexts.
05

Vocabulary & Lexical Resource

Select useful and precise language for meaning and context.

  • Range
  • Collocations
  • Chunks
  • Paraphrase
  • Register
  • Idiomatic language
Student can → choose language that fits the intended meaning.
06

Developing Ideas

Extend answers and make spoken content meaningful.

  • Examples
  • Reasons
  • Explanations
  • Descriptions
  • Comparisons
  • Consequences
Student can → move beyond short answers and develop a point.
07

Discourse & Organisation

Connect ideas into coherent spoken discourse.

  • Sequencing
  • Linking
  • Referencing
  • Topic development
  • Signposting
Student can → make extended speech easy to follow.
08

Interaction & Turn-taking

Participate actively in conversations.

  • Opening turns
  • Taking turns
  • Holding the floor
  • Yielding the floor
  • Responding
  • Interrupting appropriately
Student can → manage participation rather than only produce monologues.
09

Responding & Active Listening

Build responses from what another speaker says.

  • Acknowledging
  • Following up
  • Questioning
  • Clarifying
  • Reformulating
Student can → make responses relevant and connected.
10

Functional Communication

Use language to accomplish communicative purposes.

  • Requesting
  • Suggesting
  • Agreeing and disagreeing
  • Apologising
  • Complaining
  • Negotiating
Student can → perform common communicative functions.
11

Pragmatic Appropriacy

Choose language according to relationship, context and social meaning.

  • Politeness
  • Directness
  • Formality
  • Implied meaning
  • Social conventions
Student can → say appropriate things in appropriate ways.
12

Repair & Negotiation of Meaning

Keep communication going when understanding breaks down.

  • Clarification requests
  • Checking understanding
  • Rephrasing
  • Examples
  • Confirmation
Student can → repair communication instead of stopping.
13

Extended & Monologic Speech

Speak for longer stretches with structure and purpose.

  • Descriptions
  • Narratives
  • Explanations
  • Presentations
  • Arguments
Student can → sustain a structured spoken contribution.
14

Argumentation & Discussion

Express, support and challenge ideas in discussion.

  • Claims
  • Reasons
  • Evidence
  • Counterarguments
  • Concessions
  • Evaluation
Student can → participate in reasoned discussion.
15

Strategic Competence

Use communication strategies when linguistic resources are limited.

  • Paraphrasing
  • Approximation
  • Stalling
  • Topic avoidance
  • Compensation
Student can → maintain communication despite gaps.
16

Contextual & Register Control

Adapt speech to audience, purpose and situation.

  • Casual conversation
  • Academic speech
  • Professional interaction
  • Formal presentation
  • Audience awareness
Student can → adapt how they speak without losing effectiveness.
SPEAKING DEVELOPMENT PATH

Form → Express → Develop → Interact → Adapt → Communicate

Speaking development is not only about accuracy. Effective speakers combine accuracy, fluency, intelligibility, interaction, discourse and pragmatic control.
