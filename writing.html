01 · LANGUAGE SKILLS · 04 · WRITING

Writing

The ability to plan, construct, organise, revise and adapt written language for purpose, audience and context.

WRITING · MASTER LEARNING MAP

From sentences to purposeful communication.

Writing combines language control, idea development, organisation, cohesion, genre awareness, audience awareness and revision.

01

Writing Foundations

Build the basic mechanics that support written communication.

  • Letter and word formation
  • Spelling
  • Sentence boundaries
  • Basic punctuation
  • Legibility and digital conventions
Student can → produce readable basic written language.
02

Sentence Construction

Build accurate and meaningful sentences.

  • Word order
  • Clause structure
  • Sentence types
  • Complex sentences
  • Subordination and coordination
Student can → construct increasingly controlled sentences.
03

Grammar & Accuracy

Control grammatical forms in written communication.

  • Tense and aspect
  • Agreement
  • Articles and determiners
  • Prepositions
  • Complex structures
  • Error correction
Student can → write with appropriate grammatical control.
04

Vocabulary & Lexical Resource

Choose precise and context-appropriate written language.

  • Range
  • Collocations
  • Word formation
  • Academic vocabulary
  • Register
  • Paraphrase
Student can → express ideas precisely without repetitive language.
05

Idea Generation & Development

Generate relevant content and develop ideas sufficiently.

  • Brainstorming
  • Examples
  • Reasons
  • Explanation
  • Evidence
  • Elaboration
Student can → develop ideas rather than merely list them.
06

Paragraphing

Organise ideas into effective paragraphs.

  • Topic sentence
  • Supporting ideas
  • Development
  • Unity
  • Paragraph transitions
Student can → build focused and purposeful paragraphs.
07

Cohesion & Coherence

Make relationships between ideas clear to the reader.

  • Reference
  • Linking devices
  • Lexical chains
  • Logical progression
  • Information flow
Student can → create writing that is connected and easy to follow.
08

Organisation & Structure

Shape a complete text around a clear communicative purpose.

  • Introduction
  • Body structure
  • Conclusion
  • Sequencing
  • Overall progression
Student can → organise a text at both paragraph and whole-text level.
09

Purpose, Audience & Context

Adapt writing to who will read it and why.

  • Purpose
  • Audience
  • Context
  • Formality
  • Medium
Student can → make appropriate choices for a specific reader and purpose.
10

Genre & Text Types

Understand and produce different kinds of writing.

  • Narrative
  • Description
  • Opinion
  • Argument
  • Report
  • Review
  • Email and professional genres
Student can → use conventions appropriate to the genre.
11

Argumentation & Evidence

Build reasoned written arguments.

  • Claims
  • Reasons
  • Examples
  • Evidence
  • Counterarguments
  • Concession
  • Evaluation
Student can → support and qualify ideas logically.
12

Style, Tone & Register

Control how writing sounds and how it positions the reader.

  • Formal / informal
  • Neutral / evaluative
  • Academic style
  • Professional tone
  • Emphasis and hedging
Student can → adjust style without losing clarity.
13

Planning & Drafting

Turn ideas into an organised first version.

  • Task analysis
  • Outlining
  • Sequencing
  • Drafting
  • Time management
Student can → plan before writing rather than rely on improvisation.
14

Revision & Editing

Improve writing through deliberate review.

  • Content revision
  • Organisation review
  • Grammar editing
  • Vocabulary improvement
  • Cohesion checks
  • Proofreading
Student can → identify and improve weaknesses in a draft.
15

Source Use & Synthesis

Integrate information from other texts responsibly.

  • Note-taking
  • Paraphrasing
  • Summarising
  • Comparing sources
  • Integrating evidence
  • Citation awareness
Student can → transform source information into original, coherent writing.
16

Independent Written Communication

Transfer writing ability to real academic, professional and everyday contexts.

  • Emails
  • Reports
  • Essays
  • Applications
  • Reflections
  • Professional documents
Student can → produce purposeful writing independently for real readers.
WRITING DEVELOPMENT PATH

Plan → Draft → Organise → Develop → Revise → Edit → Publish

Effective writing is a process, not a one-pass production task. Advanced writers continually manage content, language, organisation and audience.
